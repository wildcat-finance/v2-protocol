## Purpose

This is the discussion artifact for the V2.5 pre-audit pass. It consolidates the raw multi-agent review, X-Ray output, static analysis, test evidence, known-issue reconciliation, and the fix proposals preserved in `9eeb8f8`.

The report is meant to be walked one item at a time. It separates four things that the first synthesis mixed together:

1. behavior the code actually permits;
2. whether that behavior violates an agreed protocol invariant;
3. whether the impact is realistic in the release threat model; and
4. whether the proposed patch is safe and desirable.

This is not an external audit and it is not a claim that the protocol is free of vulnerabilities.

## Correction to the preliminary synthesis

The preliminary synthesis called the wrapper access behavior a release blocker, treated disabled-template reuse as a vulnerability, and treated cross-borrower salt competition as an in-scope attack. Those classifications were wrong.

- `WKI-030` explicitly records wrapper-share composability as intentional. A borrower may permit or credential the wrapper as the market-token holder, after which non-sanctioned wrapper shares may circulate without re-running market role-provider checks.
- `WKI-009` explicitly records continued use of existing immutable hook instances after template disablement as intentional. Disabling a template stops new instances; it does not retire existing instances.
- The review scope excludes attacks that require a registered borrower to act maliciously against another borrower. The zero-prefix salt path requires exactly that actor, so it is not an in-scope vulnerability even though the address behavior is technically reproducible.

The code behavior was identified correctly, but it was compared against an invented invariant instead of the existing decisions. The earlier blanket testnet no-go conclusion is withdrawn. This report supersedes the disposition table in `x-ray/x-ray.md`; the raw X-Ray evidence remains useful.

## Candidate findings

### A-01: Zero-prefix market salts are globally reusable

Disposition: **Out of scope; requires a malicious registered borrower**  
Severity: **Not an in-scope finding**  
Raw convergence: 5 reviewers; preliminary confidence 90  
Patch state: **Accepted with a revised strict-prefix implementation after `9eeb8f8`**

Affected paths:

- [`HooksFactory.computeMarketAddress`](../src/HooksFactory.sol#L631)
- [`HooksFactory._deployMarket`](../src/HooksFactory.sol#L695)
- [`HooksFactoryRevolving.computeMarketAddress`](../src/HooksFactoryRevolving.sol#L647)
- [`HooksFactoryRevolving._deployMarket`](../src/HooksFactoryRevolving.sol#L733)

Claim:

At the baseline, any registered borrower could use a salt whose first 20 bytes were zero. The CREATE2 address committed to the factory, raw salt, and fixed market init code. Borrower, asset, hooks, and economic configuration arrived through transient constructor state and were not part of that address commitment.

If a victim announced or funded that predicted address, another registered borrower could consume the salt first with different market configuration. If the attacker chose the prefunded asset, the new attacker-operated market could borrow the balance.

Evidence:

- The baseline accepted either a caller-prefixed salt or a zero-prefix salt.
- Both callers calculated the same zero-prefix address.
- The app's normal create-market path already generates `borrower address || 12 random bytes`, so it does not depend on the zero-prefix shortcut.
- Regressions now prove that zero-prefix prediction and deployment revert, valid explicit salts remain publicly predictable, and a borrower-account salt must use the account contract rather than its principal.

Scope and counterpoints:

- The attack requires one registered borrower to act maliciously against another borrower. The owner has confirmed that malicious-borrower attacks are outside this review scope.
- The path needs another registered borrower, disclosure or discovery of the salt, and value sent to a market address before deployment.
- Counterfactual prefunding may not be a supported protocol or application flow.
- The prior zero-prefix mode may have been deliberately caller-independent.
- The accepted change intentionally removes that mode instead of making address prediction depend on `eth_call.from`.
- Existing explicit `deployer || 12-byte nonce` salts preserve their market addresses. Zero-prefix and small-integer salts now revert and must be migrated.
- The current app's direct-EOA path is already compatible because it constructs `connected address || 12 random bytes`. For borrower accounts, future callers must pass the account contract as the deployer; principal transfer does not change that namespace.

Disposition of the attempted patch:

No security remediation is required under the stated threat model. The owner nevertheless accepted strict caller-prefixed salts as defense in depth, to remove an exploitable surface and reduce predictable bug-bounty overhead. The first caller-dependent shorthand proposal was replaced: prediction is context-free, zero-prefix salts fail explicitly, the current direct-EOA app behavior remains compatible, and the protocol bytecode impact is negligible.

Downstream handoff:

- **SDK:** document the strict `immediate factory caller || 12-byte nonce` format and consider a constructor/validator helper. Do not infer the deployer from a signer because a borrower-account signer or principal is not necessarily the factory caller.
- **App:** no A-01 change is required for the current direct-EOA flow. When borrower-account origination is added, construct the prefix from the account contract that executes the factory call.
- **Subgraph:** no A-01 code change is required because the contract ABI and event shapes are unchanged.

Current implementation verification:

- `forge test --match-contract 'HooksFactory(Test|RevolvingTest)|BorrowerAccountOriginationTest|BorrowerAccountCompatibilityTest|Wildcat4626WrapperStandardTest|WildcatMarketRevolvingTest' -vv`: **163 passed, 0 failed** across six suites.
- The borrower-account compatibility suite proves that principal-prefixed salts revert while account-prefixed salts remain valid through principal migration.
- `FOUNDRY_PROFILE=deploy forge build --sizes src/HooksFactory.sol src/HooksFactoryRevolving.sol`: **passed**. Runtime sizes are 16,504 bytes for `HooksFactory` and 17,312 bytes for `HooksFactoryRevolving`, leaving 8,072 and 7,264 bytes of EIP-170 margin.
- In a controlled build comparison, the strict-prefix implementation is 23 runtime bytes smaller than the original zero-prefix baseline in each factory. Relative to the first caller-dependent shorthand proposal, it is 29 bytes larger in the standard factory and 52 bytes smaller in the revolving factory.
- A repository-wide `forge test -vv` attempt remained in compilation for 20 minutes and was stopped without a test result. This is an incomplete broad check, not a failure; the final release candidate still needs the normal full-suite/CI gate.

### A-02: Wrapper shares do not reproduce market role-provider access

Disposition: **Intentional; refuted as a vulnerability by WKI-030**  
Severity: **Not a finding under the current policy**  
Raw convergence: 2 reviewers; preliminary confidence 90  
Patch state: No patch applied

Affected paths:

- [`Wildcat4626WrapperFactory.createWrapper`](../src/vault/Wildcat4626WrapperFactory.sol#L133)
- [`Wildcat4626Wrapper.deposit`](../src/vault/Wildcat4626Wrapper.sol#L265)
- [`Wildcat4626Wrapper.mint`](../src/vault/Wildcat4626Wrapper.sol#L296)
- inherited share transfers through [`_beforeTokenTransfer`](../src/vault/Wildcat4626Wrapper.sol#L549)
- [`Wildcat4626Wrapper.redeem`](../src/vault/Wildcat4626Wrapper.sol#L368)

Observed behavior:

Once the wrapper is permitted or credentialed as the market-token recipient, wrapper shares can be minted or transferred to non-credentialed, non-sanctioned accounts. Redeeming still transfers the market token through its normal hook path to the selected receiver.

Why this is not a finding:

`WKI-030` records exactly this behavior as intentional wrapper composability. Market role-provider access controls direct market-token custody, not beneficial ownership of every derivative or venue position. Wrapper transfers still enforce borrower-scoped sanctions, and unwrap receivers still face the market-token transfer hook.

A report would need a distinct failure, such as:

- sanctioned wrapper transfers or redemptions succeeding;
- unwrapping bypassing the market-token transfer hook;
- unauthorized redemption;
- broken share backing; or
- funds becoming lost or locked outside the documented rules.

Owner decision:

Closed with no protocol change. Credentials govern who may deposit into or directly receive tokens from the market; they do not govern ownership of broadly composable ERC-4626 wrapper shares. The caller of `deposit` or `mint` is responsible for selecting the share receiver and accounting for whether that receiver can later unwrap market tokens to itself. Restricting the receiver would not close the broader path because a holder could mint to itself and immediately transfer the shares. The earlier recommendation to reject access-gated markets should not be carried forward as an audit remediation.

### A-03: `maxDeposit` and `maxMint` may advertise capacity before the wrapper is credentialed

Disposition: **Accepted low-severity ERC-4626 integration correction**
Severity: **Low**
Raw convergence: 1 reviewer; promoted confidence 75
Patch state: **Applied in the current working tree**

Affected paths:

- [`Wildcat4626Wrapper.maxDeposit`](../src/vault/Wildcat4626Wrapper.sol#L161)
- [`Wildcat4626Wrapper.maxMint`](../src/vault/Wildcat4626Wrapper.sol#L181)
- [`IMarketTransferPolicy.isMarketTransferRecipientAllowed`](../src/access/IMarketTransferPolicy.sol)
- [`BaseAccessControls._isMarketTransferRecipientAllowed`](../src/access/BaseAccessControls.sol)
- the OpenTerm, FixedTerm, and PeriodicTerm hook implementations
- [`Wildcat4626WrapperFactory._validateTransferPolicy`](../src/vault/Wildcat4626WrapperFactory.sol)

Claim:

On a credential-gated market, the wrapper can exist before the wrapper address itself is eligible to receive market tokens. The limit functions can return positive values because they check sanctions, wrapper operation, cap, and rounding, but not whether the market will accept a transfer into the wrapper. The corresponding deposit or mint can then revert.

Why this survives WKI-030:

WKI-030 addresses intentional circulation after the borrower permits or credentials the wrapper. It does not answer whether ERC-4626 limit functions should report zero before that setup is complete.

Impact:

This is router and integration correctness, not demonstrated fund loss. A caller may select or size a deposit that is globally impossible at that moment.

Owner decision:

Wrapper deployment is activation and remains permissionless. An open-transfer wrapper is immediately usable by an eligible holder. For a market whose token transfers require access, deployment still succeeds, but the limit functions should tell the truth that no deposit or mint can execute until the wrapper itself can receive market tokens. This is independent of A-02: the wrapper shares remain broadly composable after minting.

Implemented behavior:

- `IMarketTransferPolicy` now exposes a recipient-readiness view for an ordinary transfer with no extra hook data.
- The three built-in access hooks answer from the same recipient-side conditions as `onTransfer`: unrestricted transfer policy, known-lender status, deposit block, and current credential availability. Permanently disabled transfers remain rejected.
- `maxDeposit` returns zero when that readiness query returns false or reverts. `maxMint` inherits the same result through `maxDeposit`.
- `previewDeposit` and `previewMint` remain conversion-only, as required by their ERC-4626 role. No deposit, mint, share-transfer, or redemption permission was added.
- The wrapper factory probes both transfer-policy selectors before creating a new v2.5 wrapper. A custom hook that does not implement recipient readiness is not wrapper-compatible and fails closed at creation rather than producing a wrapper whose limits are permanently misleading.

Verification:

- Before the fix, the production-mirror regression reproduced a positive `maxDeposit` while `deposit` reverted because the wrapper lacked access.
- The focused wrapper suite passes 168 tests, including open-transfer immediate capacity and a conservative zero when the policy query fails.
- Both production-mirror wrapper cases pass through unavailable, credentialed, and known-lender states. Revoking the credential after the wrapper's first successful receipt does not incorrectly hide capacity because known-lender status is durable.
- A cross-template integration regression passes for OpenTerm, FixedTerm, and PeriodicTerm hooks.
- Deploy-profile runtime growth against `f79a993` is 232 bytes for OpenTerm, 232 for FixedTerm, 225 for PeriodicTerm, 193 for the wrapper, and 427 for the wrapper factory. The tightest affected contract is PeriodicTerm at 19,491 bytes, leaving 5,085 bytes of EIP-170 margin.

Compatibility and downstream handoff:

- This adds one view selector to the v2.5 hook transfer-policy interface and a corresponding probe in the wrapper factory. Custom v2.5 hook implementations must add the selector to remain wrapper-compatible. Pre-v2.5 markets continue through the legacy wrapper factory path.
- **SDK:** refresh generated hook ABIs and expose recipient readiness if it models transfer policies. Treat `maxDeposit`/`maxMint`, not previews, as the current wrapper-readiness signal.
- **App:** use limits to disable or explain unavailable deposit/mint actions. Do not gate conversion quotes.
- **Subgraph:** no event or indexing behavior changes. Refresh the hooks ABI only if the subgraph binds view methods from the shared interface.

### A-04: APR-reduction threshold is compared after flooring

Disposition: **Confirmed arithmetic discrepancy; severity is low**  
Severity hypothesis: **Low or Informational**  
Raw convergence: 1 reviewer; preliminary confidence 85  
Patch state: Present in `9eeb8f8`

Affected path:

- [`MarketConstraintHooks._calculateTemporaryReserveRatioBips`](../src/access/MarketConstraintHooks.sol#L160)

Claim:

The baseline converted the relative APR reduction to integer bips before comparing it with the 25% no-penalty threshold. For example, reducing 7,501 bips to 5,625 bips is an exact reduction of roughly 25.009998%, but floors to 2,500 relative bips and avoids the temporary reserve increase.

Impact calibration:

- The arithmetic mismatch is real.
- The bypass is less than one relative bip over the threshold.
- In integer APR terms it permits one additional absolute bip in the nearest boundary case.
- No broader reserve bypass was shown.

Proposed fix:

Compare `reduction * BIP` with `originalAnnualInterestBips * 2500` before quantizing the relative reduction. The regression test covers the 7,501 to 5,625 boundary.

Decision required:

Confirm whether the policy means an exact 25% rational reduction or a reduction rounded down to whole relative bips. Keep the patch for the former; revert it for the latter.

### A-05: Disabled templates can still serve existing hook instances

Disposition: **Intentional; refuted as a vulnerability by WKI-009**  
Severity: **Not a finding**  
Raw convergence: 5 reviewers; promoted confidence 75  
Patch state: Present in `9eeb8f8`, but conflicts with the recorded decision

Affected paths:

- [`HooksFactory.deployMarket`](../src/HooksFactory.sol#L804)
- [`HooksFactoryRevolving.deployMarket`](../src/HooksFactoryRevolving.sol#L855)

Observed behavior at the baseline:

Disabling a template prevented deployment of new hook instances. Existing immutable instances could continue serving new markets.

Why this is not a finding:

`WKI-009` records this as intentional lifecycle behavior and notes that the same claim was previously rejected. Existing hooks are immutable deployed contracts; template disablement is an instance-creation control, not an emergency revocation system for deployed instances.

Patch risk:

The proposed patch changes both factories, interface documentation, and tests so template disablement also blocks new markets through existing instances. That is a product and governance semantic change, not neutral hardening.

Initial recommendation:

Revert the A-05 portion of `9eeb8f8` unless the team explicitly reverses WKI-009 and wants template disablement to become a full origination kill switch.

### A-06: Extreme liquidity can overflow withdrawal-capacity conversion before the debt cap

Disposition: **Defensive hardening**  
Severity hypothesis: **Informational under supported assets**  
Raw convergence: 2 reviewers; promoted confidence 75  
Patch state: Present in `9eeb8f8`

Affected path:

- [`MarketStateLib.maxScaledSettleableAmount`](../src/libraries/MarketState.sol#L100)

Claim:

At available liquidity around `floor(type(uint256).max / 1e27)`, the baseline could overflow inside `mulDivUp(normalizedAmount + 1, RAY, scaleFactor)` before the caller capped the result to a `uint104` withdrawal debt.

Impact calibration:

The path could block batch settlement if a listed token and attacker could supply roughly `1.1579e50` base units. No supported asset or economically plausible balance was identified.

Proposed fix:

Return the `uint104` maximum once liquidity already covers the floor-normalized value of that maximum. This avoids multiplying an attacker-inflatable balance unnecessarily. A boundary regression is included.

Decision required:

This is not a release vulnerability under current asset assumptions. Retain it only if the team considers the added branch a low-risk improvement to a generic accounting primitive.

### A-07: Near-maximum borrowing can wrap the temporary revolving drawn calculation

Disposition: **Defensive hardening**  
Severity hypothesis: **Informational under supported assets**  
Raw convergence: 1 reviewer; promoted confidence 75  
Patch state: Present in `9eeb8f8`

Affected path:

- [`WildcatMarketRevolving._onBorrow`](../src/market/WildcatMarketRevolving.sol#L81)

Claim:

The baseline performed an unchecked `_drawnAmount + amount` before clamping to outstanding debt. With a near-maximum donated asset balance, the temporary sum could wrap and understate utilization.

Impact calibration:

The test requires a token balance close to `uint256.max`. No supported asset makes the path practical.

Proposed fix:

Clamp the previous drawn amount to outstanding debt, calculate remaining debt headroom, and add only `min(amount, remainingDebt)`. The regression proves the calculation does not wrap.

Decision required:

Treat this as arithmetic hardening, not evidence of a practical attack. Retain it if the clearer bounded addition is judged lower risk than the baseline expression.

### A-08: `satAdd` does not saturate on `uint256` overflow

Disposition: **Defensive helper hardening**  
Severity hypothesis: **Informational**  
Raw convergence: 1 unscored lead  
Patch state: Present in `9eeb8f8`

Affected path:

- [`MathUtils.satAdd`](../src/libraries/MathUtils.sol#L71)

Claim:

The generic helper added unchecked and compared only the wrapped result with `maxValue`. `satAdd(type(uint256).max, 1, type(uint256).max)` therefore returned zero instead of the cap.

Reachability:

The only production caller identified during review uses uint32-bounded timestamps and TTLs. No production overflow path was found.

Proposed fix:

Also saturate when `c < a`. The change has a direct regression and fuzz coverage.

Decision required:

This is a reasonable generic-helper correction, but it should be described as hardening rather than a protocol vulnerability.

### A-09: bytes32 metadata can lose a high-bit final byte

Disposition: **Compatibility hardening inside an accepted asset boundary**  
Severity: **Not a protocol security finding**  
Raw convergence: 1 unscored lead  
Patch state: Present in `9eeb8f8`

Affected path:

- [`StringQuery.bytes32ToString`](../src/libraries/StringQuery.sol#L8)

Claim:

The baseline length formula could truncate the final byte of a right-padded bytes32 string when that byte had only its high bit set. The review used the valid UTF-8 sequence `0xC3 0x80` as a concrete example.

Decision required:

The patch improves decoding, but it is not required to close a protocol exploit. Keep it only if supporting this bytes32 compatibility behavior is worth the extra pre-release churn.

### A-11: A removed provider can revoke a credential it originally granted

Disposition: **Refuted; no harmful capability established**  
Severity: **Not a finding**

Affected path:

- [`BaseAccessControls._revokeRole`](../src/access/BaseAccessControls.sol#L591)

Observed behavior:

`revokeRole` checks whether `msg.sender` is the credential's recorded `lastProvider`. It does not require that the provider remain in the active provider mapping.

Assessment:

The removed provider can only clear the credential it originally granted. It cannot grant new access after removal, replace another provider's credential, or bypass an account block. No privilege escalation or lender loss was found.

Decision required:

None unless provider removal is intended to revoke every residual administrative relationship immediately. The current behavior is at least as restrictive for the lender and does not justify a security patch by itself.

### A-12: Gas-hostile approved providers can defeat credential failover

Disposition: **Unverified trusted-component denial-of-service lead**  
Severity hypothesis: **Informational or Low, depending on provider trust policy**  
Raw convergence: 1 unscored lead across two functions  
Patch state: No patch applied

Affected paths:

- [`BaseAccessControls._tryGetCredential`](../src/access/BaseAccessControls.sol#L641)
- [`BaseAccessControls._tryValidateCredential`](../src/access/BaseAccessControls.sol#L709)

Claim:

Provider calls forward nearly all remaining gas. Repeated providers that consume their forwarded gas can leave too little gas to reach a later honest provider or complete the market action, despite ordinary provider failure being treated as recoverable.

Counterpoints:

- Providers are approved trusted components, not arbitrary lender-selected addresses.
- A fixed stipend can create a different compatibility failure for legitimate providers and their token or vault dependencies.
- No supported provider was shown to consume gas this way.

Decision required:

Define whether provider failure isolation is a security guarantee. If it is, build a gas-profile matrix for supported providers before selecting a stipend. If it is not, document provider liveness as part of the approval trust model.

### A-13: SphereX pre-validation calldata contains trailing bytes

Disposition: **Unverified external compatibility lead**  
Severity: **Unrated**  
Raw convergence: 1 unscored lead  
Patch state: No patch applied

Affected path:

- [`SphereXProtectedRegisteredBase._getStorageSlotsAndPreparePostCalldata`](../src/spherex/SphereXProtectedRegisteredBase.sol#L151)

Claim:

The assembly call length is larger than the canonical ABI length for `sphereXValidatePre(int256,address,bytes)`, forwarding trailing memory bytes after the logical payload.

Counterpoints:

- Solidity ABI decoders ignore trailing bytes, which explains why the repository mock succeeds.
- The deployed SphereX engine implementation is not in scope, so no rejection, authentication mismatch, or state impact was demonstrated.
- This code is inherited integration machinery rather than a newly introduced V2.5 path.

Decision required:

Confirm the deployed engine's raw-calldata expectations with SphereX or source-level evidence. Do not alter the integration based only on canonical-encoding preference.

### A-14: Unknown approved hooks can revert lender-aware lens reads

Disposition: **Read-model compatibility lead**  
Severity hypothesis: **Low or out of security scope without protocol impact**  
Raw convergence: 1 unscored lead  
Patch state: No patch applied

Affected path:

- [`LenderAccountDataLib.fill`](../src/lens/LenderAccountData.sol#L35)

Claim:

Generic hook-instance readers detect unknown hook kinds and skip built-in-only getters. Lender-aware reads cast every nonzero hook to `OpenTermHooks` and call lender-status functions unconditionally. An approved custom `IHooks` implementation without those selectors can therefore revert lender-aware single and batch lens calls.

Counterpoints:

- All currently scheduled built-in templates implement the expected access-control surface.
- Production impact requires approval of a custom template outside those families.
- The lens is a read model. No custody or market-state impact was shown.
- Internal scope guidance already treats optional hook-extension lens failures as a generic compatibility concern unless a protocol impact exists.

Decision required:

Decide whether the lens promises support for every approved `IHooks` implementation or only the built-in access-control families. Add capability detection or defensive static calls only if generic support is a real API promise.

### A-15: Unsolicited asset transfers can leave revolving `drawnAmount` above net outstanding debt

Disposition: **Unresolved accounting semantics**  
Severity: **Unrated until `drawnAmount` is defined for this case**  
Raw convergence: 1 unscored lead  
Patch state: No patch applied; A-07 does not resolve this question

Affected path:

- [`WildcatMarketRevolving._calculateRevolvingBaseInterest`](../src/market/WildcatMarketRevolving.sol#L127)

Claim:

`_drawnAmount` changes on borrow, explicit repayment, and close. A direct asset transfer can fund and execute a withdrawal, reducing lender debt without invoking the revolving repay hook. A later deposit can restore total supply while the stored drawn amount remains above net outstanding debt, potentially charging utilization APR on more than the current net exposure.

Counterpoints:

- The unsolicited transfer is real value delivered to the market and may reasonably be treated differently from borrower repayment.
- The example is capital-intensive and no profitable attacker path was established.
- Automatically treating every donation as borrower principal repayment may let third parties change the borrower's pricing state.

Decision required:

Define `drawnAmount` in one sentence for direct transfers and withdrawal settlement. If it must always track net outstanding principal, add a state-reconciliation point and a regression. If it tracks explicit borrower draws less explicit repayments, document why this intermediate state is correct.

### A-16: Account-principal transfer can precede market-principal transfer

Disposition: **Intentional partial-migration behavior**  
Severity: **Not a finding under the documented account model**  
Raw convergence: 1 unscored lead across two functions  
Patch state: No patch applied

Affected paths:

- [`WildcatBorrowerIdentityRegistry.acceptBorrowerAccountPrincipalTransfer`](../src/WildcatBorrowerIdentityRegistry.sol#L160)
- [`WildcatMarket.borrow`](../src/market/WildcatMarket.sol#L145)

Observed behavior:

During a principal migration, the registry may associate account `A` with new principal `Q` while a market operated by `A` still stores old principal `P`. Borrower-only calls continue to recognize `A`, while market sanctions remain in `P`'s namespace until that market accepts its explicit transfer.

Why this is not a finding:

`docs/Borrower Identity and Transfers.md` documents this exact partial state, the per-market acceptance sequence, and the absence of a protocol-wide coordinator. It also assigns account-internal authority migration to the approved account implementation and factory.

A distinct issue would require a production Borrower Account implementation that grants `Q` authority unexpectedly or violates its documented migration controls. No such implementation was in scope.

Decision required:

None at protocol level unless the migration model changes. Audit each approved Borrower Account implementation against this boundary before approving its factory.

### A-17: Approved account factories have durable account-registration authority

Disposition: **Explicit trust assumption, not an unprivileged vulnerability**  
Severity: **Not a finding under the documented registry model**  
Raw convergence: 1 unscored lead across two functions  
Patch state: No patch applied

Affected paths:

- [`WildcatBorrowerIdentityRegistry.registerBorrowerAccount`](../src/WildcatBorrowerIdentityRegistry.sol#L98)
- [`WildcatBorrowerIdentityRegistry.resolveBorrower`](../src/WildcatBorrowerIdentityRegistry.sol#L175)

Observed behavior:

An approved account factory can register a deployed contract account for a registered principal without a separate on-chain principal acceptance. Removing the factory stops future registrations but does not invalidate accounts it already registered.

Why this is not a finding:

The borrower-identity documentation states both behaviors directly. Factory approval represents trust in the factory's account-authentication process; durable resolution preserves already-created borrower accounts after factory removal.

Risk worth recording:

A compromised approved factory has broad and durable blast radius. That is an administration and factory-review risk, not an unprivileged Solidity exploit.

Decision required:

Keep factory approval narrow and audit every production account factory. Add principal signatures or an emergency account quarantine only if the team wants to change the trust model, not as a reaction to this scan alone.

### A-18: A lender can enter after a periodic APR response window and before execution

Disposition: **Open product and slippage decision**  
Severity hypothesis: **Informational or Low**  
Raw convergence: 1 unscored lead across two functions  
Patch state: No patch applied

Affected paths:

- [`PeriodicTermHooks.onDeposit`](../src/access/PeriodicTermHooks.sol#L515)
- [`PeriodicTermHooks._executePendingAnnualInterestBipsReduction`](../src/access/PeriodicTermHooks.sol#L705)

Claim:

After the proposal's lender response window closes, deposits remain open while the APR reduction can be executed permissionlessly. A lender can deposit at the displayed old APR and have the lower APR take effect immediately, then remain unable to queue a withdrawal until the next periodic window.

Counterpoints:

- The proposal and execution window are on-chain and queryable.
- The response window is designed for incumbent lenders, not necessarily as a guarantee to new deposits after it closes.
- Integrators may be expected to display pending APR state and apply entry slippage checks.
- Blocking deposits after the window changes market availability and may be more disruptive than the identified behavior.

Decision required:

Choose the product contract for new entrants while an executable reduction is pending: block entry, require explicit acknowledgement or a minimum APR, or treat visible pending state as sufficient. Then align contracts, SDK, and UI with that choice.

## Release-readiness conclusions

### What this review establishes

- No Critical or High severity issue has survived source, exploitability, and known-intent reconciliation.
- The earlier wrapper-based no-go conclusion was not supported by the existing policy record.
- The fix-proposal commit contains several sensible hardenings, but it also contains at least one deliberate semantic change that conflicts with an accepted decision.
- The source and test suite are substantial, and the deploy-profile size margin is currently adequate.

### Decisions needed before selecting fixes

1. Exact versus whole-bip APR threshold semantics (A-04).
2. Revolving `drawnAmount` semantics after unsolicited asset transfers (A-15).
3. Periodic-market entry behavior while an APR reduction is executable (A-18).

### Engineering gates after triage

1. Split `9eeb8f8` into accepted fixes, rejected proposals, tests, and audit artifacts.
2. Retain the approved strict-prefix A-01 hardening. Revert A-05 unless the team separately approves its lifecycle change.
3. Run focused tests for each retained patch, then rerun `yarn test` and the deploy-profile size build.
4. Refresh coverage when the current Forge/SphereX/Yul instrumentation blockers are resolved. Until then, retain the older coverage as historical evidence only.
5. Add an independent Echidna or Medusa harness if the audit schedule permits. Foundry invariants are useful but not independent engine evidence.
6. Resolve the four `FOR MAINNET` PeriodicTermHooks parameter TODOs before any mainnet release.
7. Regenerate every bytecode-derived deployment artifact from the final reviewed commit and perform a fresh locked-UI rehearsal. Do not reuse the existing Anvil ceremony output.

There is no audit basis for deploying the fix-proposal commit wholesale. There is also no current evidence for a blanket testnet no-go. The next step is item-by-item disposition, followed by a deliberately assembled release candidate.
