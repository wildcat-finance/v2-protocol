# V2.5 tranching preparation: repayment and default

- Status: R2-02 functional prototype verified and approved; broader qualification remains.
- Source baseline: `7038b5aa3f035355c773430eac1c24047bd4d7d7`.
- Scope: new V2.5 standard and revolving markets, with open, fixed or periodic hooks.
- Active milestone: [R2 plan](./v2.5-tranching-preparation-r2-plan.md) and
  [tracker](./v2.5-tranching-preparation-r2-tracker.md).

V2.5 needs a permanent default marker and optional dated repayment obligations
before deployment. The completed hook refactor provides the extension points
for a later tranche policy. V2.6 will supply the tranche vault, senior/junior
shares, waterfall and tranche-specific validation.

This is a working specification, not a description of deployed behavior or an
audit attestation. Confirmed requirements are separated from product proposals
and engineering recommendations. Committing the draft does not approve every
proposal below. Archive this document and its plan/tracker outside the repository
before the final release; retain the resulting behavior in maintained docs.

## 1. Confirmed requirements

| ID   | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| C-01 | Every newly deployed V2.5 market type has a permanent default marker, including markets without repayment terms.                                                                                                                  |
| C-02 | Default is a state marker. It does not itself close the market, accelerate repayment, change rates or open withdrawals. No default-reason field is required. Bool versus timestamp is an implementation choice.                   |
| C-03 | At the repayment date, require 100% reserves and start penalty immediately when underfunded. The requirement must take effect without a voluntary borrower parameter update or sufficient funding.                                |
| C-04 | Rate/reserve updates cannot bypass the dated 100% requirement. Both APR routes retain funding checks; automatic funded closure then stops accrual and further rate changes. No resumption or lower post-date reserve is selected. |
| C-05 | Repayment/default preserves batch durations, expiry keys, pro-rata allocation and FIFO payment order. It does not turn withdrawals into immediate individual payments.                                                            |
| C-06 | Once withdrawal is accepted, collection of the payable claim must not introduce a second hook eligibility veto. Keep expiry, funding, claim accounting and market sanctions handling.                                             |
| C-07 | The borrower initiates APR changes. Hooks see current/requested/effective market values and validate or reject them. A future tranche policy can read the vault's senior APR through its own interface.                           |
| C-08 | Delinquency and penalty cause no additional tranche behavior. There is no selected distress observer, tranche valuation freeze or extra tranche deposit gate.                                                                     |
| C-09 | An already-recorded default does not grant another repayment/grace interval and lead to a second default cycle. The marker stays set through cure and funded closure.                                                             |

The marker applies to newly deployed contracts. Existing deployed markets remain
immutable. Repayment is distinct from `isClosed`: current funded closure settles
all debts, stops future accrual and rejects further repayment. Marking unpaid
debt as `isClosed` would change those behaviors and is not the selected design.

The user's newer clarification of C-02 supersedes the earlier description of
default as closure. C-03 supersedes the explanation PDF's ordinary-grace wording.
The other session's handoff is useful input, not blanket approval of its claims.

P-01 was confirmed by the user's instruction: "if they cure at the exact block,
they cure at the exact block." Implement the comparison against
`block.timestamp`: no transaction at the cutoff may latch a new default for that
run before another transaction in the same block has a chance to cure it. Once
the timestamp is strictly later, an uncured cutoff is historical and today's
repayment cannot erase it. An already recorded earlier default stays recorded.

### Automatic closure after repayment: authorized working assumption

Once the scheduled repayment obligation is complete, the market closes
permanently. It does not resume deposits or borrowing. The user authorized this
assumption after raising the alternative of continued operation; do not retain
that alternative as an active implementation requirement.

The initial prototype reuses the funding criterion of existing `closeMarket()`:
assets cover all lender balances, including accrued interest, plus allocated
unclaimed withdrawals and accrued protocol fees. Lenders need not execute every
claim before closure. Test exact funding, one-unit shortfall, partial repayment,
unclaimed amounts and revolving principal independently.

Scheduled automatic closure applies only to markets with enabled repayment
terms, at or after `R`. Full funding before `R`, or in a market without terms,
does not itself close the market. At `R`, already sufficient historical funding
can satisfy the obligation; otherwise completion is observed through a market
update at or after `R`. A successful final `repay()` can close in that same
transaction after its explicit repayment accounting has run. A direct transfer
alone executes no market code and is not an explicit repayment operation.

The funding test counts the available cash that existing funded closure already
uses. This must not reclassify a donation as a `DebtRepaid` event or principal
repayment. Any eventual clearing of revolving principal is the separate closure
transition, as in current `_onCloseMarket`. The prototype must make this
consequence explicit in its tests and review notes.

Completion at the inclusive deadline is timely. If the obligation was missed,
judge the historical cutoff before applying a late repayment or closure. A
late full repayment may close the facility but never erases an earlier default.
Unpaid default alone still does not close or accelerate the market.

A zero-period facility may repay throughout the date's timestamp; an unfunded
deadline is judged only afterward. Zero is valid. The frontend must explain
that it provides no interval beyond the repayment-date timestamp and may be
ill-advised; this warning must not become a contract-level ban or minimum.

## 3. Terms, state and authority

Use `R` for repayment date, `P` for repayment period and `D = R + P` for the
deadline. These are independent of fixed maturity and periodic withdrawal
windows. A fixed market may become open term before `R`.

### Creation and validation

The R2-01 creation interface uses `uint32 repaymentDate` and `uint32 repaymentPeriod`,
appended to both `DeployMarketInputs` and `MarketParameters`. Both factories pass
them to the hook and the market constructor. The encoding inventory in section 7
records the exact selector changes. The decision register above supplies the
selected product rules and authorized automatic-closure assumption.

Core validation is independent of the installed hook:

- `repaymentDate == 0` requires `repaymentPeriod == 0` and disables dated behavior.
- A nonzero date must be in the future. Period zero is valid here.
- Add date and period in a wide type and require the result to fit `uint32`.
- Borrower/principal and hook-administrator transfers cannot mutate these terms.

Hooks enforce product limits using the existing parameter-validation structure.
Fixed-term initialization also checks `repaymentDate >= fixedTermEndTime` when
terms are enabled. Existing maturity updates can only move maturity earlier,
so they preserve that relation; retain a regression for this fact.

The preferred initial configuration keeps limits in the registered template's
policy and preserves the existing `(administrator, bytes args)` constructor
envelope. Sepolia and mainnet may register policies with different approved
limits. If immutable deployment configuration replaces constants, specify its
authority first: current factory constructor bytes come from the deployment
caller. Immutability alone does not prevent a borrower choosing arbitrary caps.
Never silently add positional constructor arguments without updating both
factories, deployment tooling and tests.

Constraint discovery and enforcement share one internal virtual
`_getParameterConstraints()` implementation. Keep the existing public
`getParameterConstraints()` selector and append `uint32 maximumRepaymentPeriod`
and `uint32 maximumRepaymentDateDelay` to `MarketParameterConstraints`. The
static result grows from 10 to 12 words; update the embedded lens tuple too.
Registered template policy supplies the values, never arbitrary borrower
constructor bytes. A template override changes the reported and enforced
bounds together.

Use 90 days for the period cap and retain the two-year open/periodic date cap
as the documented working network setting. The fixed policy also requires
`repaymentDate >= fixedTermEndTime`. Its draft has no additional date-delay
cap beyond the core timestamp horizon; report `type(uint32).max` for that
bound rather than advertising an unenforced two-year cap. Finalize that cap,
the fixed-term maximum and existing periodic network constants at R5. None of
these template choices changes the core's zero-period encoding or safety rules.

### Default marker and storage

R2-01 selects a stored `uint32 defaultedAt` marker, with zero meaning no recorded
default, exposed through `defaultedAt() returns (uint256)`. Do not add a second
boolean getter or reason enum. Consumers can test the timestamp for zero.

Keep lifecycle bookkeeping outside `MarketState`. It is copied verbatim into
hook calldata with size `0x1c0`. The market constructor also has a fixed response
size, currently `_MARKET_PARAMETERS_SIZE = 0x2c0`; two appended static fields
would make it `0x300`. Update every encoder, decoder and assembly dependency
together rather than changing only the Solidity declarations.

Pack the marker, run tracking and any needed completion flags into ordinary
storage where possible. Avoid the five reserved top-of-storage address slots
and map effects on inherited revolving storage. Conceptual flags may be derived
from already-persisted timestamps if tests prove event uniqueness and correctness.
Do not add a large phase enum or history log without a demonstrated requirement.

The initial prototype layout uses one packed lifecycle struct immediately after
`_withdrawalData` in `WildcatMarketBase`. Its first field is the `uint32` marker;
the run tracker uses a `uint40 penaltyCutoff` with zero meaning no active run.
Compute that cutoff in a wide type. Forty bits accommodate an episode beginning
near the existing `uint32` time horizon plus 90 days, without wrapping into an
earlier default. This does not extend the market's existing accounting horizon.
For an active run (`penaltyCutoff != 0`), the selected P-01 comparison is
`block.timestamp > penaltyCutoff`; equality still allows an observed full cure.

Automatic completion uses the existing permanent `isClosed` state. No reserve
restoration or resumption state is selected. If bounded settlement needs extra
bookkeeping, include it in the R2-02 layout/size measurement rather than claiming
that the current packed-word sketch has already proved the complete design.

Baseline layout was checked with `forge inspect ... storage-layout --json`:

| State                       | Baseline slots | Initial R2 layout |
| --------------------------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| `_state`                    | 0–3            | 0–3               |
| `_accounts`                 | 4              | 4                 |
| `_withdrawalData`           | 5–8            | 5–8               |
| New packed lifecycle struct | Absent         | 9                 |
| `allowance` mapping root    | 9              | 10                |
| Revolving `_drawnAmount`    | 10             | 11                |

These are new deployments, not a storage migration. R2-02 compiler output
confirms this layout. Production revolving assembly already uses
`_drawnAmount.slot`; the prototype updates `RevolvingDrawnAmountSlot` and expands
the borrower-transfer test's inspected range, preserving its invariants. Do not change
`MarketState` or its manually packed asset checkpoint to make room for the marker.

No administrative `setDefault` or clearing function is proposed. A marker is
latched once, using the earliest qualifying historical cutoff. Subsequent
causes, cure, authority transfers and closure do not overwrite it. Default
storage must never become an authorization gate for unrelated market actions.

## 4. Actions and transitions

This table uses selected P-01/P-03, the automatic-closure assumption in P-02,
and settled P-04 terms. “Ordinary” means existing market/term-hook behavior,
including access, reserves and sanctions. Default itself changes none of the
actions. Completed repayment closes the market; it does not restore lending.

| Action                           | No terms, or before R                                                | Active repayment phase                                                              | Funded closure                                      |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Deposit / maximum deposit        | Ordinary                                                             | Reject / zero under P-03                                                            | Existing rejection / zero                           |
| Borrow / borrowable assets       | Ordinary                                                             | Reject / zero under P-03, including surplus draws                                   | Existing rejection / zero                           |
| Queue withdrawal                 | Ordinary hook admission and batching                                 | Bypass queue admission hook under P-03; keep sanctions, balances and normal batches | Existing closed-market batching; preserve sanctions |
| Execute allocated withdrawal     | C-06: no second eligibility veto; ordinary expiry/funding/accounting | Same; no early batch expiry                                                         | Same existing funded-claim accounting               |
| Repay / bounded batch processing | Ordinary                                                             | Continue; preserve FIFO and explicit revolving principal treatment                  | Existing closed-market restriction                  |
| APR update, both routes          | Existing term policy and funding guards                              | Preserve 100% reserves; funding guard and term policy still apply                   | Existing rejection                                  |
| Collect protocol fees            | Ordinary                                                             | Preserve existing fee priority and liability accounting                             | Ordinary funded-close accounting                    |

### Scheduled reserve and penalty transition

The core applies the 100% requirement internally. It cannot call the ordinary
borrower-only setter, whose funding checks would prevent this transition when
it is needed. No new general borrower authority is granted to the hook.

The dated requirement is effective at `R`, even when the first later transaction
is delayed. Views calculate the same effective state. A successful write emits
the transition once with timestamp `R`; a reverted transaction records nothing.
Closed facilities do not restart accrual when their former date arrives.
Once funded automatic closure occurs, `isClosed` prevents another activation
or renewed accrual. A date check alone must never restart a closed facility.

Preserve the current penalty base, fee separation and rounding in
[`FeeMath`](../../src/libraries/FeeMath.sol). Do not switch to charging only the
cash shortfall, double-charge an already-penalized interval, or charge funded
withdrawal liabilities as though they were live interest-bearing shares.

Engineering recommendation: keep ordinary `timeDelinquent` advancement/decay,
and treat the full duration of an underfunded interval during the active
repayment phase as penalized, without waiting for grace. Healthy intervals retain ordinary timer/fee decay;
funded closure retains its existing stopping behavior. This avoids fabricating
pre-date delinquent time. Prove the result for both interest models before
selecting the helper implementation. Simply zeroing grace everywhere or raising
the timer can change healthy-tail economics; neither is an interchangeable fix.

Bundled APR strategies must also handle the dated reserve floor. A pre-date
temporary-reserve entry must not restore a lower ratio, and should not make an
otherwise permitted, fully funded APR update fail indefinitely. Compute the
correct effective ratio before `_checkAprChange`, retire obsolete temporary
bookkeeping consistently, and retain a core floor check. Do not silently clamp
the result after the validator has seen different “effective” values.

### Automatic funded closure: prototype requirements

Reuse the funded-close accounting and event helpers; keep one implementation
of each settlement calculation. Preserve the borrower-initiated closure path
and its permission checks. Scheduled completion must not require the borrower
to separately call `closeMarket()` or grant a new approval at the repayment date.

Existing `closeMarket()` walks the entire unpaid FIFO. Do not add that unbounded
loop to every state update. The initial design separates freezing a fully funded
facility from finishing its batch bookkeeping: stop accrual, retain full backing
for all claims, and allow bounded permissionless allocation to finish in FIFO
order. This changes the current invariant that closure has already drained every
unpaid batch. Prove claim conservation and dust settlement before adopting it.

`repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches(0, maxBatches)` currently rejects closed
markets too. If it becomes the post-close maintenance path, permit zero-value
processing explicitly while continuing to reject positive repayment after
closure. Current-batch expiry keys, post-close queueing and paid-claim execution
must remain consistent; no batch can be silently abandoned or paid twice.

Scheduled automatic closure bypasses `onCloseMarket`; manual closure retains
its existing dispatch. The scheduled obligation cannot depend on a new hook
approval. Periodic policy must consult authoritative core closure state when
reporting effective closure, withdrawal availability and pending proposals, and
when accepting new proposals. Keep raw stored data explicitly distinguished
from effective data if exposed. Do not add a mandatory observer merely to mirror
closure state. The market's `MarketClosed` event is authoritative for automatic
closure; document that the manual hook's `PeriodicTermClosed`/proposal-cancel
logs are not guaranteed on this path.

Views must agree on accrual through the effective close timestamp. Review the
stored-only `isClosed()` getter, whose existing comment assumes state updates
cannot close a market, and keep its stored/live distinction explicit. Emit
`MarketClosed` once with the operational borrower and effective timestamp;
a permissionless caller is not the borrower. Handle surplus assets deliberately,
using existing funded-close recipient semantics without taking funds from claims.

### Withdrawal collection

C-06 applies to accepted claims generally, including markets without repayment
terms. R2-01 selects removal of execution-hook dispatch from new markets. Keep
`Bit_Enabled_ExecuteWithdrawal = 93`, its existing encoder position and the
`IHooks.onExecuteWithdrawal` declaration reserved for older integrations; do not
reuse or renumber them. Supported templates continue to advertise neither
optional nor required execution dispatch.

Validate the effective `HooksConfig` returned by `onCreateMarket`: the market
constructor must reject an enabled execution bit, including one returned by a
custom hook. Checking only the caller's requested flags is insufficient. The
new market's execution path makes no call to `onExecuteWithdrawal`. This is an
intentional behavior change from the hook-refactor baseline, to be implemented
and covered by dedicated configuration and collection tests.

An unconstrained callback with a caught revert is not a proven substitute: it
can still consume gas or make payment depend on external behavior. No new
observer or bookkeeping callback is required by the current scope.

The market still validates batch/claim accounting and routes a sanctioned
recipient's payment to escrow. Direct queueing retains `_getAccount` sanctions
checks. Quarantine and token-transfer handling are distinct from collection;
do not remove their checks as a side effect of changing withdrawal dispatch.
Under selected P-03, bypass of the queue hook removes its
schedule, access and feature checks. Future policies cannot rely on this
callback for mandatory accounting while bypass applies. Automatic closure does not restore ordinary hook admission restrictions.

## 5. Default clock and repayment outcome

Under P-01, maintain a separate start/cutoff for an uncured penalized episode.
It is not `timeDelinquent`: that timer rises during delinquency and decays while
healthy, and can keep producing penalty fees during healthy decay.

- Before `R`, an uncured episode starts when ordinary grace has been exhausted.
- During the active repayment phase, an underfunded interval is immediately
  eligible. An episode already running continues; reaching `R` never restarts it.
- An observed healthy write resets only this default-run tracker. A cure covers
  the current `liquidityRequired()`; during repayment, that is the whole debt. It does
  not forgive fees, reset the economic timer or clear a recorded default.
- Under the selected inclusive cutoff, a healthy write at the cutoff can cure;
  the first later update records any earlier uncured expiry before processing
  a late cure. Multiple transactions at the cutoff must get the same opportunity.
- Snapshot/update rules also cover batch-boundary classification and zero-value
  maintenance calls. Historical transitions must not be erased by the final
  healthy classification of a later action.

The tracker must observe the existing economic intervals without splitting
interest accrual merely to emit a default marker. Adding an otherwise unnecessary
split could change compounding/rounding on markets without repayment terms.

### Repayment completion and deadline outcome

The P-02 prototype uses the funded-close criterion above. At 100% reserves,
`liquidityRequired()` equals `totalDebts()`: live normalized supply, allocated
unclaimed withdrawals and accrued protocol fees. Unpaid queued shares are
already included in live supply. Do not double-count them or add a dust waiver.

A repayment operation does not itself extinguish each lender's share balance.
On completion, automatic funded closure stops future accrual while preserving
those balances and allocated claims for withdrawal. The relevant obligation is
satisfied at its effective completion timestamp, not when the last lender
collects. Existing borrower-initiated funded closure can also satisfy it.

Judge an inclusive deadline from historical accounting once the timestamp has
passed. A later transaction's incoming cash cannot cure the earlier deadline.
Completion observed by the cutoff prevents that deadline default; completion
afterward does not clear a missed-deadline marker. The ordinary penalty-run
rule and any earlier permanent default remain independent.

For a timestamp marker, use the missed cutoff timestamp (`D`, or the episode
cutoff), while latching it only once that inclusive cutoff can be evaluated.
The timestamp identifies the missed obligation, not the block in which the
storage write happened. Do not add one to a `uint32` maximum deadline merely
to represent the first later block; preserve the existing finite time horizon.

## 6. Chronological accounting

The shared transition design must serve both `_getUpdatedState` and
`_calculateCurrentState`. Share calculations where practical; only the write
path commits storage/events. Derive timestamps from terms and interval state,
not from the keeper's arrival time. Keep work bounded, independent of elapsed
days; existing explicit bounded withdrawal processing remains separate.

### Historical cash and principal

[`_writeState`](../../src/market/WildcatMarketBase.sol) currently retains its
asset checkpoint only while a current batch exists. Extend recognized asset
tracking for enabled repayment terms, including with no pending batch. Snapshot
the final post-action balance on successful writes, with the existing saturation
bound or an equally justified representation.

Both `repay` and `repayAndProcessUnpaidWithdrawalBatches` transfer funds before
state update. Use historical recognized assets to evaluate crossed dates, and
current assets to fund today's batches. Today's cash must remain available today
without being credited to yesterday's deadline. Update the checkpoint only after
the relevant historical evaluation. Direct ERC-20 transfers enter the asset
checkpoint at a market update; the contract cannot infer their earlier transfer
timestamps. This records cash, not a repayment operation.

Current `totalAssets()` reads the raw underlying-token balance. A direct transfer
from either a borrower or lender is a donation: it mints no shares, invokes no
market repayment callback and does not reconcile revolving `drawnAmount`. Its
cash is nevertheless visible to existing reserve checks and withdrawal funding.
Existing tests preserve the distinction between donated liquidity and explicit
principal repayment. Keep that behavior; do not silently exclude donations from
ordinary market liquidity or relabel them as explicit repayment. The new dated
funded-closure transition is separately specified under P-02; observing cash
must never manufacture an explicit repayment event or principal-repayment entry.

Use matching historical debt, scale, fees and revolving drawn principal.
Revolving donations remain liquidity rather than explicit principal repayment.
Reconcile today's explicit repayment after the historical intervals it cannot
change. Reuse current commitment-fee/drawn-APR economics; do not replace them
with the PDF's shorthand commitment fee on undrawn capital.

### Boundary ordering

The first implementation milestone must lock and test the following ordering,
including automatic funded closure at the earliest recognized completion point:

1. Accrue each interval through its next logical boundary under the regime that
   applied during that interval. Derive default-run crossings from the same
   historical classifications, preserving inclusive-cure semantics under P-01.
2. Apply the repayment requirement at `R`; subsequent underfunded seconds have
   no grace. There is no fee for a zero-length interval at the transition itself.
3. Preserve batch expiry's existing strict comparison: expiry `E` is processed
   when `now > E`, not `now == E`. At a coincident `R`, activate the dated rule
   before post-boundary classification; retain normal batch payment arithmetic.
4. Evaluate an inclusive deadline from the state as of `D`, incorporating all
   recognized writes timestamped at/before it and only batch expiries strictly
   earlier than `D`. A batch whose expiry equals `D` must not be treated as
   already expired by a view of `D`. Evaluate its expiry afterward on catch-up.
5. Apply the current action and its cash/principal changes after earlier cutoffs
   have been judged. Writes exactly at an inclusive cutoff remain eligible;
   finalization waits for a later timestamp. Keep events and marker timestamps
   consistent with those rules.

When `R == D`, activation and deadline observation have no intervening accrual
interval, but all writes at that timestamp still count under P-02. Default does
not depend on which transaction was first in the deadline second. If the
reference model reveals incompatible tie rules, revise them explicitly before
production integration; do not rely on call order to choose economics.

separately, have explicit reentrancy semantics and match the write computation.

### R2-01 creation encoding inventory

`DeployMarketInputs` gains two trailing static ABI words. Its tuple changes from
`(address,string,string,uint128,uint16,uint16,uint32,uint16,uint32,uint256)` to
`(address,string,string,uint128,uint16,uint16,uint32,uint16,uint32,uint256,uint32,uint32)`.
The tuple head grows from `0x140` to `0x180`; the string tails move accordingly.

the full transient payload after deployment.
